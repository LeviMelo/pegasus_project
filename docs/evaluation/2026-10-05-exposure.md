# The exposure switch: POPSVS against population-account-2 on SIM chapter IX and SINASC births

**Question.** Does the pegasus_data account (municipality x sex x five-year band, 2010-2023, with intervals) give a better exposure than POPSVS, and does its uncertainty belong in the predictive? **Regime.** 2026-10-05, `scripts/measure_exposure.py`, artifacts `data/exposure/{ix,births}.json` (and `births_insample.json`), contiguity graph, commit 98e65e0 + working tree, 5554 common places; fits 2010-2023 (B0-B2) and 2010-2019 (BP, 2020-2023 held out).

**The two populations (`data/exposure/popcompare.py`).** The same data to 1 % in the median municipality: 2010 identical (the account's anchor is POPSVS), 2015 median |log ratio| of the municipal total 0.7-1.0 %, 2023 the account 1.4-2.0 % below POPSVS under 100,000 people and 2.7 % above in the 500,000+ (60+: -2.0 to -2.4 % under 20,000, +3.0 % in the largest). Median sd of log N: 0.021 at the censuses, 0.06-0.09 mid-period, 0.04 in 2023. The exposure's coefficient of variation per place-year is 0.026 (rho 0) to 0.061 (rho 1) against the negative binomial's 1/sqrt(phi) = 0.41 (IX) and 0.21 (births): 0.4 % to 2 % of the variance.

The block phi: IX 5.96 (account) against 6.00, births 22.6 against 22.0. **The account is not better**: 0.3-0.5 % worse deviance, a worse births BP KS (.094 against .059; both over-predict, 10.92M and 10.94M against 10.46M observed), calibration tied elsewhere.

**Exposure variance.** Added on top of the fitted phi it improves B0 (under-dispersed; IX 304,794 -> 301,519 at rho 0, 291,322 at rho 1) and leaves the field-phi version unchanged; at B1 it moves IX by -419 (rho 0) and -1,307 (rho 1) and births by +825 and +3,825; at B2 and BP by under 0.1 % (births B2 +1,220/+5,268, worse). It double counts the exposure error already inside phi (phi is estimated with mu fixed); it does not repair BP's miscalibration.

**Where the leads change** (B1 IX, popsvs against account-2 with rho 0): 168 of the 200 most upward place-years are shared (I60-I69: 171). The expectation moves by a median 0.4 % in the smallest fifth (under 4,600 people) and 0.0-0.1 % above 30,000; the surprise by a median 0.12 sd (0.23 sd for I60-I69) in the smallest fifth, 0.04-0.05 sd in the largest, and 3.4 % of the smallest fifth's I60-I69 cells by more than 1 sd. The largest changes are zero-count cells with mu of 1-3 in small places (e.g. 420895 in 2017: z -2.0 to -4.0), where a 2 % change in mu moves the Poisson tail.

**Not done.** The `popsvs-5y` control (POPSVS in the account's 17 bands) was fitted only for the training spans (births 2010-2019 phi 25.05, IX 2010-2019 phi 7.53), so source and bands are not separated; per-region KS are in the json. The first IX account fit failed (phi infinite: one death at a cell with no population, now unallocated) and was refitted.

**Verdict.** POPSVS stays the default (ADR-0010).

## What this comparison measures (coordinator's note, 2026-10-05)

**POPSVS is not truth.** It is IBGE's projection, a modelled estimate partly built from vital events, like the population account.

**The criterion.** Each candidate denominator is put into the PegaSUS model, which is scored on the OBSERVED events: SIM deaths and SINASC births. The measures are in-sample NLL, KS, and held-out years. A denominator wins if it makes the observed counts more predictable.

**What that criterion cannot see.**
- **Level error.** The model's place effects absorb a constant multiplicative error in a place's population, so the test is largely blind to it. That is the very error census-coverage correction fixes.
- **It is therefore no test of demographic correctness.** It rewards consistency over time and age structure.
- **It is partly circular for both estimates,** since both use vital events.
- **It may favour a smoother denominator** for a smooth model.

**So "POPSVS stays the default"** means only that the observed events are predicted better with it. Differences between the two estimates (e.g. women 20–44 after 2019) are disagreements, not errors of either.

**The independent reference** is the PES-corrected 2022 Census (evaluation `2026-10-05-census-coverage`). That check is assigned to the population-account front.

## Account-6 (`population-account-6`): the women 20-44 after 2019 diagnosed, births and infant fields re-scored

**Regime.** 2026-10-05, branch pegasus-core-fixes; `scripts/measure_exposure.py eval {births,xvi} ...` (`EXPOSURE_SUFFIX=_a6`; artifacts `data/exposure/{births,xvi}_a6.json`, logs `eval_*_a6.log`, `births_diag{,2}_a6.py`, `age0check_a6.py`); the population side (diagnosis, probes, the fix) is in pegasus_data `docs/evaluation/2026-10-05-population-account-3.md`, section *Timing of the residual*, and its ADR on population-account-3 (item 7). Gateway sources `account-6` (1991-2030) and `hybrid` (POPSVS, the account's age 0).

**What the two comparisons can and cannot show.** (1) The births-exposure test says which denominator makes registered births more predictable inside PegaSUS's model; place effects absorb level errors, so it rewards the time and age shape, and it is not a measurement of the population. POPSVS is IBGE's projection, built partly from the same events: "the account's women 20-44 run 1-4% above POPSVS" is a disagreement, not an error of either. (2) The only near-truth is the PES-corrected 2022 census (docs/evaluation/2026-10-05-census-coverage.md), and the account is anchored on it, so at 2022 only POPSVS is informative: women 20-44, POPSVS -2.1% nationally, +1.2% in places under 20,000, -2.3% / -3.6% at 100-500k / 500k+, +1.7% in the Northeast (median per municipality; 15-19 +3.7% in the smallest places, 45-49 +2.3%); that is a disagreement between IBGE's distribution by place size and the PES margins (the truth is a rake of published margins, modelled), and the census cannot say which is right for 2020-21 or 2023, where no census exists. Ages 0-4 give a partial arbiter (all born after 2010): against births less deaths the corrected census is 1.050 in places under 5,000, POPSVS 1.081.

**Diagnosis (population side; (a)-(e) in the pegasus_data entry).** COVID mortality of women 20-44 (excess 0.077% of the population), the 2022 to 2023 join and the post-2019 migration extrapolation (2020-22 are interior years) do not produce the gap; the 2022 level differs by place size but is absorbed by place effects. What does is that the account's cohort residual was spread **evenly through the years** while it sits at ages 15-27 in both decades (small places lose 3-4% of a cohort a year at 18-22): the account/POPSVS ratio of places under 20,000 rose to 1.019 (2016) and fell to 0.990 (2022). `population-account-6` times the residual by an age schedule of migration (ratio: 0.996 in 2016 and 2019); confirmed on the Contagem 1996 by age, out of period (bias at ages 15-29 of small areas from about 2% to under 1%).

**Births (common places; NLL, block phi; BP = held-out 2020-23).**

| POPSVS | 343,524 | 334,658 | 99,405 | .059 |
| account-3 | 344,937 | 334,773 | 99,862 | .096 |
| **account-6** | 344,263 | **334,277** | 99,589 | .081 |

The loss at B1 halves (+1,413 to +739) and the held-out loss shrinks from +457 to +184; B2 is won (-381); the field-phi B0 is won (388,273 against 388,513). 2010-19 now carries +117 (was +384) and 2020-23 +621 (was +1,029; 2021 +193, 2022 +234, 2023 +138), still in places of 50-250 births a year (+426 of 621): their women 15-49 are 0.98 of POPSVS in 2022 (1.00 in 2015), the census anchor. **Account-6 does not beat POPSVS on held-out births.** Its remaining loss is the 2022 level in 2,500-100,000-people places, which is a disagreement with POPSVS the data cannot settle (the PES margin for small places is 3.9%; at ages 0-4 the corrected census is nearer the births than POPSVS). The diagnostic with its own phi (`births_diag_a6.py`) gives B1 -201 and BP +285, so B1's sign depends on the dispersion treatment; B2 and BP lead the same way.

**Age 0 (`age0check_a6.py`; the account uses those events, so it is not independent).** Median |log ratio| to births less infant deaths: 2015 POPSVS 0.122, account-6 0.037 (mid-year reference 0.117 / 0.015); 2020 0.114 / 0.045 (0.098 / 0.032); POPSVS about 10% high in the median municipality in both years, the account within 1%; the account is closer in 83% / 80% of municipalities.

**Infant fields, perinatal conditions (SIM chapter XVI, deaths at age 0 are nearly all of them; `eval xvi popsvs account-6 hybrid`).** NLL, block phi (KS in brackets):

| | B1 | B2 | BP | BP KS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| POPSVS | 113,645 (.010) | 112,329 | 31,596 | .006 |
| account-6 | **113,432** (.006) | 112,311 | 31,579 | .005 |
| hybrid (POPSVS + account age 0) | 113,433 (.006) | 112,312 | 31,579 | .007 |

The account's age 0 is better on every tier (B1 -213 = -0.19%, B2 -18, held-out BP -17 = -0.05%) and better calibrated at B1; the hybrid equals account-6 to within one unit, so the whole difference comes from age 0. The gain is small in the held-out span and the age-0 reference is not independent of the account's inputs, but the predictive score is.

**Recommendation.** POPSVS stays the general default (ADR-0010): the account-6 loss on births stands. For fields whose exposure is the newborn (SIM deaths under 1 year, chapter XVI, SINASC-linked infant fields) the **hybrid** is better than POPSVS and loses nothing elsewhere (adult bands are POPSVS's); it is available as `PEGASUS_POPULATION=hybrid` and `gateway.population(..., source="hybrid")`, and is not made a default here. Not done: age 1-4 in the hybrid (POPSVS's 1-4 may be high in small places too; unchecked), the hybrid on births (women's bands are POPSVS's, so it equals POPSVS), the exposure-variance (`rho`) variants for account-6.

## Hybrid as the default for newborn-exposure fields (ADR-0010 amended)

`monolith.default_population` reads the block's age-0 share from the cached event counts (`NEWBORN_SHARE` 0.5) and picks `hybrid` or `popsvs`; the keys carry the choice. Check (2010-2023, `monolith.default_population`, commit of this entry): age-0 share 0.994 for SIM chapter XVI (292,941 deaths) so `hybrid`, 0.001 for chapter IX (4,990,893) and 0 for SINASC births so `popsvs`. XVI's default key equals the explicit hybrid one (`population: hybrid`, `population_model: population-account-6`) and the stored hybrid fit loads through `Monolith.load` without a source; IX's key is unchanged (no population part), so its fits are untouched.

# ARCHITECTURE 4.1 exposure and groups: completeness κ, SUS-dependent N, race groups (ADR-0020)

**Question.** Does the expectation μ = κ · N^{(v)} · exp(η) improve with the completeness of the system and, for SIH, with the population without private coverage, and can race enter the groups? **Regime.** 2026-10-05, branch design-v0; `data/exposure/kappa_proxy.py` (artifact `kappa_proxy.json`), `scripts/measure_exposure.py` targets `ix`, `births`, `sih_ix`, `births_r{1,2,4}`, `xvi_r{1,2,4}`; stored POPSVS fits (SIM IX and births 2010-2023, B1).

## Stage 1: completeness κ (SIM, SINASC by UF × year)
- **Exposure.** `popsvs+kappa` multiplies POPSVS by κ: the total is 0.939 of POPSVS in 2010, 0.958 in 2019, 0.966 in 2023 for SIM (SINASC ≈ 0.94-0.95); s (σ of log N) 0.02.
- **Refit-free test** on the stored B1 fits (the real test, refits `popsvs+kappa` × full/train, is queued behind the heavy slots, below): (1) the UF × year log(observed/expected) of IX, centred by UF and by year, regressed on log κ: slope 2.3 (bootstrap over UFs 95 % 1.1 to 3.9), correlation 0.62, 378 cells; births: slope 6.1 (1.6 to 8.5), correlation 0.15 (κ barely moves after 2011). A slope above 1 says the residual moves more than κ alone (κ is correlated with the ill-defined-cause share). (2) μ · κ_{UF,t}, renormalised by UF level and national year (what geography and history would absorb), NB negative log-likelihood of the place-year totals at the block φ: IX 245,679 to 245,372 (-306, 0.12 %): Norte -71, Nordeste -237, Sul -50, Centro-Oeste -2, Sudeste +53; births 343,490 to 343,452 (-37). The North's observed/expected rose 15 % between 2010-12 and 2021-23, the Southeast's fell 2 %. A first count of surprises above z = 3 by region used a different z from the stored one and is discarded.
- **Reading.** κ explains part of the UF × year drift of the North and Northeast (the regions where completeness rose) and nothing elsewhere: what ADR-0010's POPSVS-versus-account comparison could not do. Not adopted as a default until the refits are read; opt-in.
